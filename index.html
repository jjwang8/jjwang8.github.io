Research

My research centers on rethinking the primitive building blocks of deep learning architectures, and how new designs change what a model can learn and at what cost. Humans can learn a new card game from a few examples, revise a plan mid-execution, and transfer knowledge across domains; today's models, despite their scale, largely cannot. I'm drawn to the architectural mechanisms that might close that gap, rather than to scaling existing designs further.

My first-author work, PolyNeXt (ICML 2026), is one instance of this: it replaces standard nonlinearities (ReLU, GELU, softmax) with activation-free polynomial, Hadamard-product modules that match or exceed activation-based vision backbones at lower compute. One of its lessons is that depth, not width, drives expressiveness: stacking narrow layers buys exponentially richer functions at only linear parameter cost. I'm extending this architecture-first program toward new attention mechanisms and layer primitives, redesigning the internals of how large models are built rather than treating them as fixed.

That depth-over-width lesson also points past simply stacking deeper networks. More broadly, I'm interested in alternative architectural designs that let a model reason and deliberate in a learned latent space, using recurrence to add reasoning depth rather than by stacking more layers. World models are one example of this, learning structured internal representations a model can predict and plan over.

Experience

Researcher, Chrysos Lab, UW-Madison

Advisor: Grigorios Chrysos · Oct 2024 – present

First-author work on PolyNeXt (ICML 2026): a family of CNN- and transformer-style backbones (PolyMLP, PolyConv, PolyAttn) built from activation-free polynomial (Hadamard-product) units in place of ReLU and softmax. The models reach 84.3% top-1 on ImageNet-1K at 26M parameters and scale to 85.2%, matching the activation-based state of the art (CAFormer) at equal size while staying fully activation-free, and surpassing prior polynomial networks by ~2-3% at less than half the FLOPs and 30-45% less peak GPU memory than comparable MetaFormer models, with strong out-of-distribution robustness (ImageNet-C/A/R/Sketch) and ADE20K segmentation transfer. A fully-polynomial (BatchNorm) variant uses only adds and multiplies, a step toward FHE-compatible inference.

My senior honors thesis extends this line, showing empirically that block-recurrent depth compression, which works well on trained ViTs, fails on polynomial networks: their depth computes genuinely distinct polynomial terms rather than redundant refinements.

Evaluating Modality Bias in Multimodal LLMs

UW-Madison, Jan – May 2026 · project page

Built a counterfactual-ablation pipeline to measure how multimodal LLMs (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, Gemma-3n) weight visual versus textual evidence, with custom metrics (Attribution Faithfulness Score, Lexical Override Rate, Text Override Rate). Models misreport their own modality use and flip 69% of correct answers under adversarial text injection; modality-separated prompting and importance scoring did not reliably beat baselines, suggesting fixes require architecture or training changes rather than prompting.
